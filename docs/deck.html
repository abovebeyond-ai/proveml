Claims carry their bindings.

what the model writes

Hi Marta, @[order:4821]{order 4821} ships on %[shipDate]{14 March}, and your warranty runs until %[warrantyUntil]{3 June 2026}. ?[covered: IS_IN_WARRANTY]{You are still covered}, so the repair is free.

what the reader sees, every mark computed

Hi Marta, order 4821 ships on 14 March, and your warranty runs until 3 June 2026. You are still covered, so the repair is free.

A subject names its record. A value binds to a field. A verdict binds to a declared threshold, never to the model's opinion. Strip the markup and clean prose remains; the receipt keeps the bindings.

Nothing unproven ships: the gate refuses the page before a reader ever sees it.

job: most audiences have seen a hundred architecture diagrams and zero syntax; showing the actual marks is the proof that a thing exists. Green is machine-verified against the record, amber is a verdict held against a registered threshold. say: "the export ships without the markup; the bindings live in the receipt." Twenty seconds, no grammar lesson. Colour law: green means proven, dashed means a verdict held against a declared rule, dotted means a value held against a record. Amber is reserved for the human layer everywhere in this deck. trap: teaching the syntax. Nobody needs to parse it; they need to see it is real.

A verdict may only bind to a rule that is declared here: one field, one comparison, one value, one label, one source. Small enough to read aloud. Total enough to always answer. A missing rule is a refusal, never an improvisation.

Exact means signed. stayDays arrived inside the credential; the model never touched the number. Signed field, declared rule: the machine closes it alone, and the agent moves on.
The policy itself stays prose. Nobody turned "listed nationals may stay ninety days" into code. The model reads it and proposes the binding; a person signs that reading once, and the table grows a row.

The interpreter is as unbounded as language. The judge is provably total: every check terminates, every check answers. This is all of it.

A different object, in good company.

Every hard part is borrowed from giants: Markdown’s inline marks, Certificate Transparency’s trees, git’s content addressing, SD-JWT credentials from the IETF, DIDs from the W3C. Only the composition is new. The neighbours sign something else:

C2PA signs a file's provenance: who made the media and how it was edited. Its object is the container; ours is the claims inside a text and the human judgement of them.
E-signatures attest a whole document as one unit: integrity for the blob. We work one field finer, so a value carries its own proof and its own reading.
RAG citations point to sources, useful context in the loop. We add what a link does not: a verbatim check, survival through regeneration, and a signature.
LLM guardrails are a second model's opinion at runtime, valuable in the loop. Alongside them we leave a receipt a third party can re-check.

ProveML signs the claims and the judgement: value granularity, evidence bound, with survival rules for every yes.

job: place yourself on their map before they place you, and generously. "Is this C2PA?" is coming; the answer is "closest cousin, different object: they sign the container, we sign the claims and the human judgement of them." say: lead each row with what the neighbour does well, then our different object, never "worse"; several of these are allies, not rivals. The title itself signals it: in good company. trap: trash talk. Standards people remember who was gracious about neighbours.

The yes is a key, not a promise.

  • Every archived source folds to one signable root under a versioned canonicalisation contract.
  • A human judges each reading, yes or no, next to its proof.
  • Each yes is keyed to the fingerprints of its block and the blocks beside it. Meaning is not block-local.
  • The judgements, plus the output's own root, fold into one review root, signed back as a credential. Flip a judgement, change a word, touch a source: the signature no longer matches.
  • So a downstream policy can say: a field the agent wrote is admissible input, if it arrives under a review root. There are only two ways a value gets there: byte-proven from a signed source, or signed off by a person. There is no third way in.

Regenerate the document: only what moved comes back. Human judgement, memoized.

job: the deepest novelty in plain words, and the best attack answered before anyone raises it. Nobody else makes human sign-off incremental; "memoized" makes the CS people feel it. say: "meaning is not block-local, so the key is not either: change the frame above a figure and the yes dies with it." also say: "checked by anyone, trusted from no one." And the loop-closer for the agent folks: a policy can now admit an agent-written field as input, not because the model is trusted, but because provenance or a person stands behind every value under the root. Admissibility, not truth: the word "true" stays out of it. What we do not claim.

  • Fidelity, not truth. The chain certifies faithful quotation, never the wisdom of source selection.
  • Atoms, not narrative. Prose stays prose, and readers judge prose as prose.
  • Revocation and expiry: envelope-layer roadmap, not yet built.
  • Canonicalisation is unaudited, and versioned precisely so it can be replaced.

The demand is here. So is the on-ramp.

  • Regulated environments: human oversight of AI is becoming mandatory, and today it is a checkbox nobody can verify. This makes it a review someone can check.
  • Reliable agents: an agent is only useful acting across organisations, on data it did not make. It can demand the review root before it acts, so autonomy becomes a property you can check, not a setting you toggle.
  • Adopt without waiting for anyone: embed a zero-dependency module between a model call and a screen, or install (ˆ◕ˆ)⎕ Vera in the tools people already write with. No new app, no two-sided market.
  • Full value at zero network adoption: unsigned sources still archive and prove; every signed source only makes a receipt stronger.
